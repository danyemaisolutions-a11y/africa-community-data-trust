# Proposed community-centred governance framework

**This council is a design concept only; no council has been constituted, elected, or delegated authority.**

## Status

The structures and processes below are proposals for discussion. No Community Governance Council, community representation process, research approval body, complaint channel, or formal governance mandate is established by the current repository. This framework does not itself confer legal authority or ethics approval.

Governance arrangements should be co-designed with affected communities and relevant institutions. DanYem may act as a technical steward, but must not be presented as the sole authority over community data or research approvals.

## Proposed Community Governance Council

A Community Governance Council could provide collective oversight of ACDT's governance principles and proposed use of its technical infrastructure. Its mandate, membership, decision rights, resources, and relationship to institutional research ethics bodies would need to be agreed before it is established.

Potential responsibilities for consultation include:

- Reviewing proposed data uses, purposes, and community impact.
- Advising on acceptable access conditions and benefit-sharing expectations.
- Reviewing transparency reports, complaints themes, and unresolved risks.
- Recommending whether a proposed use should be escalated, revised, or declined under an agreed process.
- Periodically reviewing whether the governance framework remains appropriate.

The Council's role must be defined in relation to, not as a substitute for, participant choices, applicable law, institutional ethics review, or other legitimate community decision-making arrangements.

## Community representation

Any council or review process should:

- Include representation chosen through a transparent and locally appropriate process by the communities concerned.
- Consider diversity of affected groups, language, age, gender, disability, and relevant lived experience without assuming one representative speaks for everyone.
- Make membership terms, selection criteria, quorum, decision records, and routes for raising concerns clear.
- Provide accessible information, interpretation where needed, and reasonable support for participation.
- Identify and manage conflicts of interest.
- Revisit representation when a project, community, or data-use context changes.

No specific representatives, communities, or partner organisations are confirmed by this proposal.

## Participant rights and decision-making

Proposed system and project processes should explain, in understandable and accessible terms:

- What data is proposed to be collected or used, for what purpose, by whom, and for how long.
- What choices are available, what is optional, and what consequences follow from declining.
- How a participant can ask questions, access relevant information, raise a concern, or withdraw consent where applicable.
- Which uses, including secondary research or AI-related use, require separate consideration and consent where required.
- The practical limits of withdrawal, including whether data has already been lawfully used, aggregated, or shared.

Participation in community governance does not replace an individual participant's rights or choices. No participant workflow is currently implemented.

## Researcher and institutional responsibilities

Researchers and institutions seeking access should be expected, under any future approved framework, to:

- State a specific purpose, methods, data needs, risks, recipients, and retention period.
- Demonstrate relevant ethics review and other required approvals before access.
- Use only approved data and access methods, and not attempt re-identification or unauthorized linkage.
- Protect data, report incidents promptly, comply with agreed restrictions, and securely dispose of data at the end of the approved period.
- Report results and relevant findings back in an agreed, accessible form.
- Document and deliver agreed community benefits, and cooperate with monitoring and audit.

These are proposed expectations, not current contractual or technical controls.

## Technical steward responsibilities

A technical steward could maintain the software and infrastructure under documented, community‑approved rules. Responsibilities should be bounded by a defined mandate and include:

- Implementing only approved policies and making material changes reviewable.
- Protecting systems and credentials, documenting incidents, and supporting independent assessment.
- Providing understandable records of system operation and access decisions.
- Avoiding unilateral decisions about community data uses or research approvals.
- Disclosing relevant conflicts and separating technical operations from approval authority.

DanYem may contribute technical stewardship, but this proposal does not assign it sole governance authority.

## Conflicts of interest

People involved in a decision should disclose financial, institutional, personal, or other interests that could affect impartiality. A future process should define recusal, independent review, conflict registers where appropriate, and how conflicts are communicated to affected communities.

## Transparency and accountability

Subject to privacy, safety, and legitimate confidentiality constraints, a future programme should publish or share:

- The governance mandate, membership and decision process.
- Approved purposes and access conditions.
- Aggregate records of access decisions, incidents, complaints, and remediation.
- Benefit‑sharing commitments, progress, and reasons for material changes.
- Reviews of whether consent and access conditions were respected.

Records should be understandable and accessible, while avoiding disclosure of personal data or details that create safety risks.

## Benefit-sharing and community feedback

Benefit‑sharing should be discussed with affected communities before research access is considered. It may include non‑monetary, capacity‑building, infrastructure, or other locally determined benefits, without treating benefits as a substitute for consent or a justification for unacceptable risks.

## Complaints and dispute resolution

A future framework should provide a safe and accessible route to raise concerns, acknowledge and track complaints, define response timelines and escalation, protect complainants from retaliation, and document outcomes. Where appropriate, disputes should have an independent review path and access to relevant institutional or legal remedies. No complaints or dispute‑resolution mechanism is currently implemented.

## Establishment conditions

Before this framework is treated as operational, affected communities and relevant institutions should review and agree its mandate, representation, decision rights, safeguards, resourcing, accountability, and relationship to formal ethics and regulatory processes. The proposal should receive qualified Nigerian data‑protection and legal review.
